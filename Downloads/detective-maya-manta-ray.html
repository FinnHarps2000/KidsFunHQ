The Case of the Missing Manta Ray

📍 VANCOUVER AQUARIUM
Maya's class is on a school trip to the Vancouver Aquarium when the announcement crackles over the speakers: "Manta the manta ray has disappeared from her tank." The whole aquarium goes into lockdown mode. Handlers are running. Someone dropped a clipboard. A child is crying about a gift shop penguin. It is absolute chaos. Maya puts on her detective hat. 🪼 Jellyfish Gallery — Clue 1 of 5
🪼
Glowing Witnesses!
🧒‍♀️
Maya walks into the jellyfish gallery and immediately forgets what she was doing. They're glowing. They're all just... gently glowing. In purple and blue. Maya stands there for a full twelve seconds doing nothing useful at all.
🧒‍♀️

🐧 Penguin Cove — Clue 2 of 5
🐧
Doug Has Feelings About Fish.
🧒‍♀️
Maya arrives at Penguin Cove. The keeper looks frazzled. "We're trying to feed them but the fish won't divide evenly and DOUG is being very dramatic about it." One penguin at the end is staring at the wall with his flippers crossed. His name tag says DOUG.
🐟 The keeper has 26 fish to share equally between 4 penguins. How many does each penguin get? And how many fish are left over?
🌟 Division = sharing equally. But sometimes things don't divide perfectly!
1
Share 26 fish into 4 equal groups. Ask: 4 × ? = something close to 26? Count: 4×6=24. So 6 fish each!
2
4 groups of 6 = 24. We had 26. So 26 − 24 = 2 left over. Those 2 fish are the remainder!
3
So 26 ÷ 4 = 6 remainder 2. Check: (6 × 4) + 2 = 24 + 2 = 26 ✅
4
Doug gets 6 fish. Doug is still not happy. This is about something deeper than fish, and Maya does not have time to unpack it.
🧒‍♀️
The keeper hands Maya a note found near the exhibit. It mentions Manta heading toward the shark tunnel. But first: one more division clue! Doug watches Maya go. His expression says: "I too am investigating something. My own feelings."
🧮 45 fish are shared between 9 penguins. How many each? (Division = sharing equally — the reverse of times!)
1
45 ÷ 9 — think backwards: 9 × ? = 45. Count in 9s: 9, 18, 27, 36, 45. That's 5 jumps!
2
So 45 ÷ 9 = 5. Fact family: 9×5=45, 5×9=45, 45÷9=5, 45÷5=9.
9 × 5 = 45 5 × 9 = 45 45 ÷ 9 = 5 45 ÷ 5 = 9
Division is sharing equally! For 26÷4: how many 4s fit in 26? 4×6=24, so 6 each with 2 left over. For 45÷9: think 9×?=45!

The Belugas Have No Respect.
🧒‍♀️
Maya arrives at Beluga Bay. The three belugas are called Luna, Nanuq, and Steve. Steve is eyeing Maya in a way that suggests he has done this before. A clue card is taped to the observation window.
🐳 The beluga tank holds 24 buckets of water. The keepers have refilled 3/8 of the tank today. How many buckets is that? (3/8 of 24 = ?)
🌟 Finding a fraction of a number!
1
3/8 of 24. The bottom number (8) tells us how many equal groups to split into. So: 24 ÷ 8 = 3. That is one eighth (1/8).
2
The top number (3) tells us how many of those groups we want. So 3 × 3 = 9. That is three eighths!
3
Shortcut: divide by the bottom, multiply by the top. 24 ÷ 8 = 3, then 3 × 3 = 9!

💦 SPLASH! Steve the beluga soaks Maya's notebook. She stares at him. He stares back. He does not look sorry.
🧒‍♀️
"Steven." Maya says it very calmly. Steve blows a small bubble. This is not an apology. This is a statement. Maya finds another clue card — a dry one, mercifully — tucked behind the tank.
🧩 There are 20 fish in the feeding bucket. Luna eats 1/4 of them and Nanuq eats 2/5 of them. How many does Luna eat? (1/4 of 20)
1
1/4 of 20: divide 20 into 4 equal groups. 20 ÷ 4 = 5.
2
We want 1 of those groups, so the answer is just 5. Luna eats 5 fish!
For fractions of amounts: divide by the bottom number first, then multiply by the top. For 3/8 of 24: 24÷8=3, then 3×3=9!
🔬 Discovery Centre — Clue 5 of 5
🔬
Where IS Manta?!
🧒‍♀️
The head keeper, Dr Tanaka, is in the Discovery Centre surrounded by clipboards. She looks simultaneously relieved and embarrassed. "We may have... found Manta," she says. "She was behind the large decorative coral the whole time. She does this sometimes. She likes it back there. We call it her little holiday."

🌟 Three numbers, four facts!
1
Any three numbers that multiply together form a family. Example: 4, 6 and 24. Because 4 × 6 = 24!
2
From those three numbers you get four facts: two multiplication and two division. They all use the same three numbers!
4 × 6 = 24 6 × 4 = 24 24 ÷ 4 = 6 24 ÷ 6 = 4
3
The rule: if you know one fact, you know all four! Multiplication and division are just the same family, going in different directions.

🐟
Case Closed — Manta Found!
🧒‍♀️
Maya filed her report. It read: "Manta Ray found behind coral. No foul play. Manta was simply having a moment. Estimated duration: 4 hours. Suspects: none. Investigation cost: several solved maths problems, one soggy notebook (Steve), one slightly terrifying shark tunnel."
Dr Tanaka thanked Maya with a lifetime pass to the Vancouver Aquarium and a stuffed manta ray toy. Doug the penguin, upon hearing the case was closed, turned back around to face the group. Progress.

Maya took the bus back with her class. When her teacher asked what she'd learned, she said: "Multiplication is the same as times. Division is just sharing. And Steve the beluga is not to be trusted near open notebooks."

The teacher gave her full marks. For the maths. Not for the Steve comment. Though honestly, the Steve comment was also correct.
