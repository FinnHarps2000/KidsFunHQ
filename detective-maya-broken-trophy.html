🌱 Mistakes are golden. That's how we grow.

🚪 The Hallway — Clue 4 of 5
🚪
Build the Fraction!
🧒‍♀️
In the hallway outside the trophy cabinet, Maya finds a repair notice from the caretaker. It describes how much of the trophy still needs fixing. The caretaker, it seems, is also a fan of fractions. This school is something else.
🎨 The notice says: "2/5 of the trophy pieces have been found." Build that fraction by shading the correct number of tiles below! Then answer the question.
🌟 Fractions = parts of a whole!
1
The bottom number (denominator) = how many equal parts the whole is split into.
2
The top number (numerator) = how many of those parts you're talking about.
3
2/5 means: split into 5 equal parts, and we have 2 of them. Shade exactly 2 out of 5 tiles!
Shade 2 out of 5 tiles to show 2/5
0/5
Almost! The bottom number tells you the total tiles. The top tells you how many to shade. For 2/5: shade exactly 2!
🧒‍♀️
The notice continues: "Of the 20 trophy pieces total, we have found 3/4 of them." Maya does a quick calculation. "So... 15 pieces. Five are still missing. This is becoming a very expensive accident."
🧩 3/4 of 20 pieces have been found. How many is that? (Divide by the bottom, multiply by the top!)
1
Divide by the bottom: 20 ÷ 4 = 5. This gives you 1/4 (one quarter).
2
Multiply by the top: 5 × 3 = 15. This gives you 3/4 (three quarters).
3
So 3/4 of 20 = 15. Always: ÷ bottom, × top!
Nailed it on the retry! Fraction master in progress. 🌱
For fractions of amounts: divide by the bottom first, then multiply by the top. 3/4 of 20: 20÷4=5 (one quarter), then 5×3=15 (three quarters)!
📋 Principal's Office — Clue 5 of 5

The Real Mystery.
🧒‍♀️
Theo has come to the principal's office himself. He didn't wait to be caught. He walked in, sat down, and said: "I did it. By accident. I should have said straight away. I'm sorry."
👩‍💼
The principal stares at him for a long moment. Then: "Thank you for telling me, Theo. That took courage."
🧒‍♀️
Maya watches from the doorway. She had spent all morning solving maths clues to find something Theo could have just... said. But she's not annoyed. Because watching him walk in there? That was the hardest puzzle of the day.
The real case was never the trophy. It was the fear of being wrong. Theo thought admitting a mistake would make everything worse. It made everything better.

Case Closed!
🧒‍♀️
The trophy was repaired. Theo helped put it back. His name went on a small card next to it that read: "Restored after an accident. Reported honestly by Theo, Year 4." He thought it was embarrassing. Most people thought it was the coolest thing on the whole shelf.
What Maya learned on this case:

Every wrong answer she got today taught her something. Every mistake she spotted — in the grid, in the division, in the logic — made the next step clearer. Mistakes aren't the opposite of learning. They ARE learning.

Theo figured that out too. Just took him a little longer. And that's completely fine.
